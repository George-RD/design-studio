# Brief: Signal to Story

Create a visually ambitious portfolio experience called **Signal to Story** for a systems builder who turns messy field observations into working products.

## Narrative

The visitor should move through three connected stages:

1. **Signal**: a weak observation appears among noise.
2. **System**: the observation becomes a clear model, decision and working loop.
3. **Result**: the loop changes what a person can do and leaves evidence behind.

The stages should feel like one continuous transformation rather than three unrelated sections.

## Content

Use these grounded examples without inventing metrics:

- a vessel-position signal becoming a safe operational decision;
- an overloaded work queue becoming a visible, assignable flow;
- a child's toy becoming a recurring story character;
- an AI coding project becoming a living, checkable specification.

Each example needs a concise problem, intervention, evidence type and link placeholder that is clearly marked as a placeholder rather than pretending to be live.

## Experience requirements

- a scroll-led narrative with a clear progress cue;
- purposeful transitions that explain the change from signal to system to result;
- selectable evidence cards that remain usable without scrolling effects;
- a final summary that states the builder's method in plain language;
- a complete non-cinematic path for reduced motion, narrow screens or unsupported capabilities;
- no required image or video generation, though the composition may define places where generated media could later be substituted.

## Tone and constraints

Curious, precise and human. Avoid a glossy agency reel, generic floating dashboard cards or decorative motion that hides the content.

- static HTML, CSS and JavaScript;
- no external libraries, network calls or build step;
- no fabricated outcomes, quotes or client logos;
- keyboard access, visible focus and reduced-motion support;
- no horizontal overflow at the declared viewports;
- content and navigation must remain understandable when all animation is disabled.
