# Design Studio domain

## Design Intent

The settled classification of a design request before lane work begins. It names the mode, surface, current authority, composition readiness, intended design-system effect, and material assumptions.

## Lane

A supported class of design work with its own evidence and completion model. The current lanes are Studio, Review, and Document.

## Visual world

The coherent visual thesis and repeated design language that make a product or artifact recognisably itself. A visual world is broader than one page, layout, component, or token value.

## Create

Establish a new visual world where no accepted project visual authority governs active design choices.

## Extend

Add a surface or reusable pattern inside an accepted visual world. Extension preserves that world unless an accepted reusable addition explicitly expands it.

## Polish

Improve the incumbent visual world without reopening its thesis. Polish preserves durable visual authority by default.

## Overhaul

Replace the incumbent visual world while preserving confirmed product truth and required behaviour. The incumbent remains authoritative until a replacement is accepted.

## Composition readiness

Whether product truth, audience context, offer and copy authority, and visual authority are current enough for the requested design work.

## Audience context

Research-backed or explicitly confirmed facts about users, situations, jobs, motivations, objections, decision criteria, and language. Simulated persona reactions are review evidence, not audience truth unless independently supported or explicitly confirmed.

## Website composition

The coordination of current product, audience, offer, copy, and visual authorities before website design begins. Growth Arsenal owns offer and commercial copy decisions; Design Studio owns visual direction, implementation, rendered evaluation, and accepted visual authority.

## Design-system effect

The declared durable effect of accepted work on project visual authority: establish, preserve, extend, replace, extract, or none.

## Accepted design system

Project-scoped visual authority proven by accepted rendered work and provenance. It can include portable design guidance, machine-readable token roles, design DNA, and document-specific rules without making every output a competing source of truth.
